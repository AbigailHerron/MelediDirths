You're doing just fine — writing a little each day, staying on top of your content. Then, all of a sudden, you're just… not interested anymore.

You know you should keep going. You start calling yourself “lazy.” You might even begin to wonder if your project is worth it at all.

That’s burnout. And it sucks.



I’ve been stuck in this phase for about a month now. No content creation, no replying to people on social media, no writing.



So, how did I get here?



Honestly? I’m not sure.



I didn’t feel like I was overextending myself. If anything, I stopped writing while I still had gas left in the tank. And yet, here I am.



I think what first got to me was the sameness of Twitter. It became hard to engage with anyone when everything felt like part of the same cycle. I started to wonder if anyone — myself included — really meant what they were saying.



Then Calm Volume 4 hit a roadblock. I wasn’t happy with the direction it was going in. I rewrote the beginning and… still wasn’t entirely satisfied. Reading it back now, I’m not sure whether I’ll stick with it or start again.



While I was struggling with that, Instagram carousels started slipping. I missed a day. Then a week. Then three.



It was like everything I’d invested in — everything I’d loved — just… faded. Like a quiet void growing where there used to be joy in the small things.



So, what did I do?



Nothing.



I stopped posting. Stopped writing. I just let myself be.



I tried going back to hobbies I used to enjoy (gaming, watching TV) but I could only focus for about thirty minutes at a time.



It took a few weeks before I could really enjoy those small things again.



And now, after giving myself time to rest, I’ve found my way back here—writing this.



I’ve reread my work. It’s not bad. It’s just… not quite right either. I’m not sure I have the energy to dive back in yet, but the fact that I’m here, saying any of this at all, feels like a good sign.



The good news in all of this? No one really knows me yet, so I’ve been able to rest in peace.



Long may my insignificance last.
